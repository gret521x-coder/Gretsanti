How to play

Everything below is Pindutan, the game in the first chair. The two empty chairs will get their own instructions the day they get their own game.

The board

Blocks fall one piece at a time. Fill a whole row and it clears; clear more than one row at once and you send garbage over to your rival's board, where it arrives as a row of blocks with one hole in it. The last board still standing wins. If your own stack reaches the top with nowhere to land, you are out.

A piece you do not like can be held with C, which swaps it for the next one — once per drop, so holding twice in a row is not a way out. The panel beside the board shows what is coming next.

The four ways to play

  • SOLO — your board, nobody else's. No ranks, no garbage, just the stack.
  • VS CPU — the same board against the computer, at Beginner, Intermediate or Kupal. Each level thinks faster and makes fewer mistakes than the one before it.
  • 1v1 — a real person. You play on the left, your rival's board sits beside yours at a smaller size, and garbage crosses in both directions. This is the only mode that earns rank.
  • GROUP — up to four in the room, everyone at once, last board standing wins. A group win lifts no rank, by design.

The controls

On a keyboard: ← → move, ↑ or X rotate, Z rotate back, ↓ soft drop, Space hard drop, C hold, E the Pindutan burst, P pause.

On a phone: the four pads under the board — left, rotate, right, and drop — plus the pause button. The GLIDE panel beside them sets how smoothly the piece slides when you hold a direction: 38 ms by default, with presets from instant to slow, so it can match whatever your thumb expects.

The room

One lobby for everyone. You give your name once, at the gate, and after that the only place it shows is your own card — no box to change it, nothing repeating it beside Watch. Tap a card to see that player's wins, best score, win rate, the run of wins they are on and when they were last seen; tap ⚔ to challenge them to a 1v1. There is a chat under the list, and a Watch pill under the player count if you would rather spectate.

The ladder

Ten names, no more. Ranks run Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond, three tiers each, three stars to a tier and a fourth star that steps you up a tier. Your own row reads (you). Everything on that board was earned in 1v1 — a group win lifts nothing.

Score, and the run you are on

Your card keeps the score of your best board, the win rate worked out from what you actually played, the run of wins you are on and the last time you were seen. There is no losses column — the page counts what you won, not what you lost.

Clearing several rows at once is worth more than clearing them one at a time, and the garbage that comes out of a big clear is bigger too, so a single well-placed piece can turn a losing board around. Spending the whole game clearing single rows is how a stack quietly gets away from you.

Sound

There is a sound switch in the lobby row for anyone playing somewhere quiet, and it remembers what you chose. The storm always announces itself with the fanfare at the moment the key is taken — that is the one sound that is meant to be heard over the top of everything else.

A few things worth knowing

  • Do not chase a hole. If a column already has one, filling beside it is usually cheaper than trying to dig it out later.
  • Keep the middle low and the edges tidy; the piece you are given next is always on the panel, so leave a slot for it.
  • In a 1v1, a flat board clears more often than a tall one, and every clear you take is garbage you did not have to send by hand.
  • Pause is P, and auto-pause is on by default — a phone that goes to sleep does not lose your board for you.

The storm

When the maker holds the key, the whole room lights up: three jagged bolts across the page and a fanfare at the moment the key is taken. It is lightning, not decoration — it is drawn as vector bolts, so it stays sharp on any screen, and it never falls as a straight line.

Themes

Eight, and the one you pick sticks: Neon, Sunset, Forest, Royal, Pink, Mono, Space and Christmas. Space sends satellites and ships over the page on arcs and climbs; Christmas snows across the whole page and stops at the top of the board, so the table itself stays clear to play on.

Updates

This hub reads labs/pindutan-build.json on the main branch before it opens a game, so the arena you get is always the build that file names — never an old copy that happened to be uploaded somewhere. The hub keeps itself current the same way, against the same file, and the game does the same again once it is running. Nothing here has to be re-uploaded for a new build to reach you.

No signal? Say so and stay put — the strip turns amber, the chairs still answer, and Pindutan still opens the build it already knows about.

This page holds no game code. It reads labs/pindutan-build.json, then opens the build named there, and it checks for a newer hub of its own on every open and on every tap of the strip.
